04 Five flocks, five paths

Pick an origin. Own its consequences.

Talon Banner

“Peace through pecking order.”

The military option. Cheaper soldiers, richer raids, and diplomats famous for entering with a banner and leaving with the grain.

Moonmolt Covenant

“Read the omens in every molted feather.”

The mystics. They consult the moon before breakfast, and the moon has surprisingly firm opinions about zoning.

Cogwing Guild

“Measure twice, cluck once.”

The engineers. Machines that run beautifully, provided nobody asks which gear is load-bearing.

Golden Peck Consortium

“Every sunrise is a market opening.”

The economists. They discovered grain left alone becomes more grain, and now they nap professionally. The ledger keeps counting.

Featherlight Communion

“A calm roost weathers every storm.”

The pacifists. They hold that most wars end when somebody finally shares the snacks. Their enemies leave confused and faintly blessed.

Each origin owns one core system and one unique building, and you choose once per town. The town spends the rest of history being that town — so choose like it's permanent, because it is.

05 The flock has names

The town grows. So do its Joes.

John, Alex, Dawson, and Matt are four of the twelve founders. Assign them, promote them, watch them collect skills, friendships, moods, and grudges — no two saves agree on who ends up in charge.

  • Four ranks, honestly earned. Worker, Foreman, Steward, Elder — promotions are paid in corn and stone, and an Elder lifts the whole town.
  • Perks are chosen. Traits are earned. You can respec what you picked for a Joe. You can never respec what they did.
  • Moods with consequences. An Inspired Joe outworks a Weary one, and the ledger will happily show you the difference.

06 The suggestion box got an upgrade

The flock files petitions.

Named Joes bring the town's business to your desk — specific, sincere, and slightly unhinged. Approve one and the town actually builds the spa, funds the dig, or settles the feud over a shared meal.

Declining costs nothing. Joe Town never punishes you for saying "not yet." The chicken will simply remember.

09 Defense is a puzzle

Battles are won in the planning.

Scout a rival and the report writes your battle plan: which gate is weak, which is guarded, what's worth carrying home. Pick an entry, an objective, and how much of the army to risk — then watch it play out on seeded dice you can't sweet-talk.

  • Named captains earn renown. Captains like Sir Peckard and Henrietta Boldwing get better with every campaign, and it shows on the field.
  • Three gates, three objectives. Rams love the Main Gate. Archers prefer the Cliff Path. Choose accordingly.
  • Defeat is survivable. A failed raid comes home as “a tactical learning vacation.” The flock recovers. The story continues.

10 The coopconomy

Twelve businesses. One very proud egg.

Found ventures that run themselves and pay in full whether you visit or not — the Market Stall fills in about twenty minutes, the Starship Prospectors in two days. Check in hourly or weekly; the game refuses to care. Everything banks at full value and stays yours. Forever.

  • Worm Farm — “Worms in, worms out. Mostly out.”
  • Market Stall — “Two crates and an opinion about prices.”
  • Quarry Consortium — “Several hens agree about a rock.”
  • Courier Run — “Nothing travels faster than a chicken with a parcel.”
  • Shipping Fleet — “Bulk. Simply bulk.”
  • Starship Prospectors — “Somewhere out there is a very rich rock.”

A traveling merchant rotates one-of-a-kind artifacts through town. Most do something remarkable. One is the Unremarkable Egg: “It is an egg. Nothing happens. The flock is very proud of it.” It still costs 120 food and 60 wood.

11 Leave. It's fine.

The town takes notes while you're gone.

Close the lid mid-shift and the flock keeps working. Come back in an hour or a week and you get a Welcome Back report — production, arrivals, anything waiting at the gate. Raids never resolve without you: the walls hold until you're ready to answer.

No streaks. No energy meters. No login rewards. No guilt. The only thing that accumulates while you're away is corn, and the Chronicle — the running history of a town that remembers whose idea the hole was.

13 The practical matters

Buy it once. Keep the whole game.

What kind of strategy game is Joe Town?

A native Mac city-builder and simulation. Paint roads, tune production chains, make permanent technology choices, and keep an eye on the neighbors — the ducks have a parliament, and the geese consider volume a negotiating skill.

Is $9.99 the full game?

Yes. One purchase, all ten ages, 24 buildings, 18 technologies, every named Joe, local saves. No ads, no in-app purchases, no gameplay tracking. As the flock puts it: “Hoarding, but civic-minded.”

Is Joe Town an idle game?

No — it respects your absence without demanding it. Town-defining choices wait for you; offline progress is bounded and arrives as a tidy report. No streaks, no energy meters, no guilt. The chickens simply kept busy.

Does it play offline?

Entirely. Joe Town saves locally and runs with the internet off. On your return the town reports in — usually something like “Productivity occurred without supervision.”

Which Macs are supported?

macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon or Intel, one native universal app. The chickens cannot tell which chip you have and are at peace with that.

Is every town the same?

No two are. Worlds are seeded, the twelve founders arrive in a different order every game, and towns diverge by what their Joes asked for. Two towns at the same age can disagree about nearly everything, including the theatre.

Does Joe Town collect data?

The Mac game collects nothing — no gameplay data, no accounts. This website uses Google Analytics for aggregate visits; see the privacy notice. The only census in Joe Town counts chickens, and it lists eggs as residents, pending.
